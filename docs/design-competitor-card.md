# Design: Competitor Card — "make one like their winner" (image-first, staged)

Mode: Startup

## Problem Statement
The brief's "What Mello would do" cards are account-numbers-only (by design). Separately, Selfmade already watches the founder's competitors and decodes their ad patterns (the market playbook). What's missing is the JOIN with intent: a distinct card that says "your rivals are winning with X — here's the ad, made for your brand, ready to run." The founder asked for: **ask first (check their ads, or create for me) → if create, build from the rival's winning ad (image = clone with the user's brand; UGC video = similar script/setting/model) → then run it.**

## The honest constraint (drives the whole design)
**We cannot see a competitor's real performance.** Meta's Ad Library shows *what* they run, never ROAS/spend. So "winning" is a **public-signal proxy**, not their truth:
- **Longevity** — ads live a long time (advertisers kill losers fast) → strongest signal.
- **Duplication** — many near-variants of the same concept → they're doubling down.
- **Still live** — currently active, not a stale creative.
Rank watched-competitor ads by (still-live × days-running × variant-count) → the "winning ad" candidate. The card must *say* this is inferred from how long/hard they run it, not claim their ROAS.

## Status Quo
Today the founder: reads the playbook card (aggregate patterns), then manually goes to Spy/Discovery, finds an ad, hits Remake, picks their brand. Multiple steps, no "winning" ranking, no auto-run. The competitor videos weren't even playable until this session.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- **User:** a Selfmade founder watching ≥1 competitor, with a brand + product photos, and (for "run it") a connected Meta account.
- **Wedge:** one card — "Your rivals' winning ad right now is this. Make it yours." → one click → an on-brand **image** ad built from the rival's winning concept → prefilled into the launch flow to run.

## Premises (agreed in session)
1. "Winning" is inferred from public signal (longevity + duplication + still-live), NOT competitor ROAS. The card is honest about that.
2. **Image clone is reliable today; a true UGC-video match is not.** v1 leads with image; video = "make a *similar* UGC from their hook/format/offer," not a pixel-clone of their model. Full UGC-match is Phase 2.
3. **"Run it" never auto-spends.** Create → prefill the M4 launch flow → founder sets budget + confirms → live.

## Approaches Considered
### A: Image-first, staged (CHOSEN)
Card ranks the rival's best-signal ad. Ask → "check their ads" (browse in Spy) or "create for me." Create + image → clone with the user's brand via the existing clone-image pipeline. Create + video → similar-UGC from the pattern. "Run it" → prefill M4, confirm budget. Reuses clone-image + M4 + the playbook + the reasoning engine. S→M effort, low risk.
### B: Full spec now
Image clone + true UGC-video match (their exact script/setting/model) + auto-run, all at once. Most literal to the original words, but video-match quality is a coin-flip (~$6/attempt) and auto-run risks real spend on a bad clone. L effort, high risk of "feels broken."
### C: Advice-only card
Just the insight + a link into Spy to remake manually. Smallest/safest, proves the join is useful, but doesn't "act" as asked.

## Recommended Approach — A, staged

**Phase 1 — the card + image path + prefilled run (~M).**
1. **`/api/meta/competitor-moves`** (server, read-only): watched competitors from `followed_brands` (spied) → their ads from `discovery_ads_index` (with `discovery_creatives` for media, `created_at`/last-seen for longevity, format/hook/offer/emotion tags, image-vs-video). Rank by the winning proxy. Cross-reference with the account's best segment (from `auditAccount`) to prioritize a rival ad aimed at a segment that also converts for the user. Return the top 1–3 winning ads + why ("live 47 days, 6 variants — they're scaling this").
2. **`CompetitorCard`** on the brief (its own card, below "What Mello would do"): shows the rival's winning ad (playable, via `HoverScrubVideo`), the plain-English why, and two buttons: **"See their ads"** (→ Spy/Discovery, the rival) and **"Make it mine →"**.
3. **"Make it mine"** → if the winning ad is an **image**: route to the existing clone flow (`/studio?src=asset&img=<url>` or the clone-image API) with the user's brand → on-brand image ad. If **video**: route to the studio "similar UGC" flow seeded with the hook/format/offer (not a model-match).
4. **"Run it"** → the created creative drops into the **M4 launch flow prefilled** (creative + copy from the pattern); the founder picks budget + audience and confirms → launch via the existing `MetaClient.launchFullCampaign`. Never auto-spend.

**Phase 2 (later):** true UGC-video match (transcribe the rival's script → transcreate for the user's brand → matched setting/model via the video pipeline); competitor-launch alerts ("Rival X just launched 9 ads pushing % off — here's the counter"); learn which cloned ads the founder actually ran/kept.

## Data sources / reuse
- `followed_brands` (spied competitors) + `discovery_ads_index` + `discovery_creatives` (their ads + media + tags + longevity).
- The market-playbook computation (already aggregates hook/format/offer across watched brands).
- `auditAccount` (the user's best segment, to prioritize relevant rival ads).
- Clone-image pipeline (`/api/discovery/clone-image`) + the studio remake flow.
- M4 launch (`src/lib/meta/client.ts` `launchFullCampaign`, the `/m4` flow) for "run it."
- The Phase-2 reasoning engine (`answer.ts`) to phrase the "why this rival ad" join.

## Open Questions
- "Winning" ranking weights (longevity vs variant-count vs recency) — tune on real data.
- Does the card show ONE winning ad or a small ranked set? (Recommend one hero + "2 more" to stay decisive.)
- Video "similar UGC": seed the studio with the pattern only (v1), or attempt script transcreation (Phase 2)?
- Multi-competitor: pick the single strongest signal across all watched rivals, or one per rival? (Recommend strongest-across-all for the hero.)

## Success Criteria
- The card surfaces a genuinely long-running rival ad (spot-check: is the "winner" actually live and old, not a fresh launch?).
- "Make it mine" produces an on-brand image ad in one flow (no manual brand/product picking).
- A founder can go rival-winner → cloned ad → M4 prefilled → live, in one session, with budget confirmed by them.
- Wedge proof: ≥N founders click "Make it mine" and actually launch within 2 weeks. If they browse but never create, the "create for me" thesis is weaker than "help me pick."

## Dependencies
- `discovery_ads_index` longevity/variant fields (verify created_at + last-seen are populated for watched brands).
- Clone-image pipeline + studio asset-remake (done).
- M4 launch flow + connected Meta account (done, live).

## The Assignment
Before we build the ranking, pull your own watched competitors' ads and, by hand, pick the one ad you'd bet is their winner — then write down *why* (how long it's been up, how many variants, the hook). Do it for 3 rivals. That's the labeling set for the "winning" proxy: if your gut pick matches "longest-running + most-variants," the proxy is right; if it doesn't, tell me what signal you used instead, because that's the real ranking rule.

## What I noticed about how you think
- You spec'd the *branching* first — "ask if they want to check their ads or create for me" — before the generation. That's a product instinct: you're protecting the founder who wants to decide, not just the one who wants magic. Most people design the happy path and forget the "let me look first" user.
- You were specific about the creative fork — "if image clone that, if UGC take similar script/setting/model." You already know image and video are different problems. That's why staging (image now, UGC later) will feel right to you rather than like a cut corner.
- You keep pulling the thread back to "and then run ads for them." You're not building a creative tool, you're building an operator. The whole session has been that: audit → recommend → act. This card is the same shape pointed at competitors.
