Selfmade · making the S a mark, not a letter

The difference between an S and a symbol.

A smooth continuous S is just a font glyph — anyone can type it. A mark earns a second look: the S is built from parts, has a break, or has direction baked in. Here’s the plain version you saw, three ways to give it a system, and the filled “Channel” S for comparison — all in your bold, rounded, cream-on-orange language.

Honest read: the plain S (00) is the one that felt like “just an S” — correct instinct, drop it. Of the marks, 04 · Channel is still the strongest symbol: it’s filled and bold like an app icon, the S is discovered in the gap rather than typed, and it owns a shape (orange split-S) no one else has. If you want to keep the stroke character of the bracket icon instead, 01 · Broken-waist is the best of those — two hooks with a real break, so it reads as two parts, not a letter. My recommendation: go with Channel as the symbol and let the bracket live on as a secondary/animated motif.
